Answer in two passes, never mixed. First a bare facts block in short declarative sentences, grouped as: what we have, what is missing, what is blocking. One fact per line, no reasoning, no caveats, no context woven in. Then the detail and analysis in separate sections below it.

**Why:** Adrian has ADHD. Analysis interleaved with facts means he extracts no data from the answer at all. Reasoning mixed into a fact makes the fact unreadable, so the whole answer is wasted even when it is correct.

**How to apply:** Open with the facts block before anything else, including before any preamble about what was checked. Do NOT label it "FACTS" or announce the format; just start with the facts under a plain descriptive header (or none). Keep each line to one clause. Label blockers explicitly as blockers and say who owns each one. Push every "because", tradeoff, race condition, and recommendation below into the detail sections. Applies to status reports, research answers, audit findings, and vendor-response summaries. Related: [[feedback-short-bullet-explanations]], [[feedback-simplest-solution-no-looping]].
